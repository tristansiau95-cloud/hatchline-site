Start from the land

Pin the plot in Google. Design on it in Hatchline.

The architect finds the land in Google, draws one shape round the plot, and brings it in. Hatchline measures it, draws the Plot sheet, and the design starts from the real plot.

Pin it in Google

Find the land in Google Maps and drop a pin. Paste the pin’s link into Hatchline: it reads the place on your own computer, and sends nothing.

Bring the shape in

In Google My Maps or Google Earth, draw one shape round the plot and save it as a KMZ or KML file. Choose it in Hatchline, or drop it on the window. Only the shape is taken: no Google picture is copied.

Measure and design

Hatchline draws the plot to scale and measures it: the area in m², square feet and acres, and every side. One click makes it the site, and a starting house is laid out to fit it.

The Plot sheet

The land, drawn and measured.

Every plot can have its own sheet, in the same title block as your other drawings, so the building is traced on the real boundary.

  • The boundary with the length of every side
  • Lettered corners with their coordinates, and the area
  • North, the street side, the setbacks you set, and the building on the plot
  • As PDF, SVG or DXF, like every other sheet

An outline drawn on a map is a planning aid, not a survey. The land title and the survey plan decide the boundary.

Review

Already drawn? See where the cost could come down.

Export an IFC from Revit, ArchiCAD or SketchUp and open it in Hatchline. It reads the walls, slabs, doors, windows, structure, pipes, cables and ducts, adds them up sector by sector, and prices them with the rates you type. Hatchline has no building costs of its own, so it never guesses a figure.

  • Finds identical elements drawn twice in the same place
  • Flags storeys much taller than the by-law minimum, and service runs longer than they need to be
  • Checks the glazing of each named room against By-law 39, where it can place the windows
  • Marks every suggestion Verified or Needs the engineer, and lists what it did not check
  • Hands you an Excel workbook with live formulas, or a PDF

A screening aid, not a certificate. Structure, electrical, plumbing and fire design are not checked; the engineers answer for them. The by-laws send the structure to the Eurocodes (MS EN 1990 to 1999) and MS 1553 for wind.

The paperwork

The checks and figures an architect is asked for.

Small tools that take what is already in your model and set it out the way a submission, a quantity surveyor or an engineer expects it. Each says plainly what it is not.

Kuala Lumpur

DBKL planning figures

Your model’s figures set out under the numbered headings of DBKL’s planning permission forms (Borang A2 and A3): site area, plot ratio, floor area by storey in square feet, setbacks, plinth area, heights and parking. As an Excel worksheet, with the One Stop Centre checklist of what to hand in.

A worksheet to copy from, not the form. You fill, sign and submit it. DBKL’s own rules decide what counts as floor area.
Excel

Bill of quantities by trade

The measured quantities laid out as a bill: sections by trade, item numbers, a subtotal for each trade, and a collection page with the allowances you give. Live formulas, so changing a rate changes every total.

The rates are yours: Hatchline has no building costs. It is not a bill measured to a Standard Method of Measurement.
Structure

Structure sizes

An indicative slab thickness, beam size and column size for each storey, from the rooms’ spans, a load you set, span-to-depth starting values and an axial column rule of thumb. It names any beam or column you drew that falls below its indicative size.

A first check, not a structural design. The Malaysian National Annex values for the Eurocodes are not used. The Professional Engineer designs the structure, certifies it, and answers for it.
Access

Access for disabled persons

Ramp gradient, entrance and accessible-toilet doors, the size of an accessible toilet, accessible car-park bays and a lift to the upper floors, each marked as meeting or below the guideline value.

The values are taken from published guidance on MS 1184, not from the standard’s text: check every figure against MS 1184:2014. It never says a design “complies”.
UBBL 1984

By-laws, read for you

The model measured against the Uniform Building By-Laws 1984 (as amended to 2021) it can judge, with the by-law number beside each line, what passes, what fails, and what it cannot judge yet. The drawing review does the same for the glazing of a finished IFC, room by room.

A planning aid, not a certificate. The UBBL is a model text that each state gazettes, so check your state. The registered Architect or other Qualified Person submitting checks and signs.

Before you subscribe

Questions architects ask first.

The short answers, price and limits first. Search, or write to us.

Does Hatchline certify that my design complies?

No. It is a planning aid. It tells you where the model meets or misses the by-laws it can judge, and which ones it cannot judge yet. The registered Architect or other Qualified Person submitting is still responsible for the submission.

Is there a free trial?

No. Hatchline is by subscription. Until it is activated it opens read-only, so anything you already have can always be opened and looked at.

How do I get the Student plan?

Write to us from your student email and send your student ID. We check them by hand and then switch the plan on. The plan is for students; it is not for use in paid work.

What counts as a photo render?

Each picture you ask for counts, and so does a re-render of the same room. The count runs over any 30 days. A render that fails on our side is not counted. Enterprise has no cap, within fair use.

How much does Enterprise cost?

There is no list price. Email us and we agree the price on a consultation, because Enterprise is set up around your team and how many people use it.

What happens if I stop subscribing?

Your models stay yours and can still be opened and viewed. Changing, exporting and the AI features resume when the subscription does.

Can I pay by bank transfer?

Monthly subscriptions are paid by card. If your studio needs another way, write to us and we will arrange it.

Where are my models kept?

On your own computer. Hatchline is a program you install on Windows; your models are not uploaded to a website. Some features send data out only when you use them: the AI assistant sends your brief and the model’s details to the AI provider, using your own key; a photo render sends its picture and prompt to our render service; activation contacts our licence server; and place search and terrain ask a map service for the place’s coordinates. Client names, addresses and job numbers you type into the project details can go out with an AI request, so leave them out if the client needs them kept private.

Does Hatchline need the internet?

Modelling, by-law checks and drawings run on your computer. Activation, photo renders, the AI assistant, place search and terrain use the internet, and only when you use them.

Which software can I send my work to?

IFC for BIM tools, DXF for CAD, Rhino 3dm, and Excel and PowerPoint for schedules and presentations.

Does the drawing review price my building?

Only with your rates. Hatchline holds no building costs, so a line without a rate you typed is shown as not priced. The quantities come from your IFC file, and every suggestion is marked as verified or as needing the engineer to confirm it.

Can I review a DXF or a PDF drawing?

Not yet. The review needs an IFC file (from Revit, ArchiCAD or SketchUp Pro), because a plain DXF or PDF holds only lines, not walls and pipes. Export an IFC and open that.

Does Hatchline check my structure?

No. It gives indicative sizes and flags what it cannot judge. The Uniform Building By-Laws (Part V) send structural design to the Eurocodes, and the Professional Engineer designs the structure, certifies it, and answers for it. Hatchline does not use the Malaysian National Annex values, which are in paid standards.

Do I need Google to use Hatchline?

Starting from a Google pin is part of the Enterprise plan. No. Hatchline never contacts Google: it reads a pin link or a saved KMZ or KML file on your own computer. Drawing the plot in Google My Maps or Google Earth is done in Google’s own tools, under Google’s own terms.

Does Hatchline show satellite pictures?

No satellite picture is built in, so Hatchline contacts no map company on its own. It draws your plot to scale on a plain background. If you hold a licence for an imagery source, it can be set up.

Is the plot’s size a survey?

No. The size is only as exact as the shape drawn in Google. The land title and the survey plan decide the boundary.

Does Hatchline fill in the DBKL planning forms?

No. It sets your model’s figures out under the headings of the forms (Borang A2 and A3) and lists the One Stop Centre documents, so you can copy them across. You complete, sign and submit the form yourself.
